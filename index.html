Product. Wholesale. eCommerce. Fintech. Not by accident — because each stage taught me something the next one required.

I started in product, where I learned how demand is engineered before a sale ever happens — how pricing signals value, how assortment shapes choice, and how decisions made upstream determine everything downstream.

I moved into wholesale, where I learned what happens to revenue when distribution gets complex. Working with iconic brands across the top retail channels, I saw where margin leaks, where execution breaks, and how brand equity compounds or erodes depending on how you go to market.

Then eCommerce, where the customer tells you everything in real time. Conversion, cart value, lifetime value, retention — these stopped being metrics and became the language I think in.

Today in fintech, I help digital-first businesses access the working capital they need to grow — and I build the onboarding, utilization, and expansion systems that turn a financial product into a long-term partnership.

Every stage. Same focus. Find where it's breaking — and fix it.

The Journey

Most see one piece. I've built across all four —
and that's what unlocks the full picture of how growth works.

01
Product Development
Where every commercial decision begins
Product taught me that every commercial outcome starts upstream. IMU targets, margin architecture, seasonal planning, vendor allocation — these aren't operational details, they're revenue strategy. A wrong assortment decision doesn't show up immediately. It shows up three months later — when it's too late to fix.
What I learnedRevenue starts long before the sale.
02
Wholesale
Where strategy meets the reality of scale
Wholesale taught me the real mechanics of growth at scale — open-to-buy, sell-through velocity, markdown strategy, and channel conflict. I learned what it actually takes to execute across 1,000+ doors without losing margin or brand equity along the way.
What I learnedScale reveals every weakness in the system.
03
eCommerce
Where the customer finally gets to talk back
eCommerce taught me how to lead with data and think in systems. The real skill isn't collecting customer signals — it's translating conversion trends, demand forecasting, and promotional performance into decisions that move the entire business. Data without action is just noise. Data with action is growth.
What I learnedCustomers tell you everything. You just have to listen.
04
Fintech
Where trust becomes the product
Fintech taught me the hardest truth in business: a great product means nothing if the customer never fully uses it. Onboarding friction and adoption gaps aren't product failures — they're customer experience failures. I learned to diagnose them, build the fix, and turn a financial product into something customers genuinely depend on.
What I learnedThe best growth is built on trust, not just transactions.
What I Do

The problems I solve best

01
Revenue Growth & Customer Expansion
The problem isn't the product. It's the system.
  • Customers aren't activating fast enough
  • Expansion is inconsistent
  • Retention is leaking
I dig into the data, ask why, and build the frameworks that close the gap — onboarding, incentive architecture, lifecycle strategy. I don't just find the problem. I build the fix.
What you get
Customers who activate faster, expand consistently, and stay longer.
02
Commercial Strategy & Go-To-Market
Most go-to-market problems aren't marketing problems. They're clarity problems.
  • Positioning that doesn't resonate
  • Pricing that signals the wrong value
  • Channels that don't reflect how customers actually buy
I start with the customer, work backwards through the data, and build the strategy that makes growth feel inevitable instead of forced.
What you get
A commercial foundation that creates pull instead of requiring push.
03
Cross-Functional Operations & Execution
Strategy without execution is just a conversation — I've spent my career living in the gap between what leadership decides and what the organization actually delivers.
  • Teams pulling in different directions
  • Performance data that's invisible until it's too late
  • Good intentions that never become consistent results
I align teams around one version of the truth, make performance visible, and build the accountability structures that close the gap between strategy and what actually gets delivered.
What you get
An organization that moves with clarity, speed, and commercial discipline.
04
Strategic Account Partnership
The biggest revenue opportunities are inside the relationships you already have.
  • Accounts underleveraged because nobody asked the right questions
  • Relationships that stay transactional instead of becoming strategic
  • Opportunities missed because trust was never built
I invest in understanding what each account needs to win, show up with data before it's asked for, and build trust from the operator to the boardroom. People do business with people they trust.
What you get
Accounts that don't just renew — they expand, advocate, and grow with you.
